ESP32-S3 car emblem housing

A startup project to turn a round ESP32-S3 display into a programmable emblem for a car. I designed and 3D-printed the housing that holds the display board and its wiring, and refined the fit over several iterations.

  • Built around a 2.8 in round IPS display module (480 × 480, ST7701S driver, 3.3 V analog supply).
  • Bezel holds the glass; the back has standoffs for the board and clearance for the ribbon cable and wire harness.
  • Fit checked against a 3D scan of a vehicle trunk lid.

Protective Safety Mat

A trunk-stowable tri-fold mat for changing a tire at the roadside. It gives the user a padded, insulated place to kneel and a wide base for the jack on gravel, dirt, or ice. Designed with a team of six for MEC325, guided by six personas ranging from an older driver with knee arthritis to a one-armed veteran.

  • Recycled-tire SBR body, polyurethane foam kneeling panels with a UV coating, and TPU air bladders that level the mat on uneven ground.
  • Detachable jack blocks with hook-and-loop attachment and rigid polyiso cores; reflective edge strips for night visibility; QR code linking to step-by-step instructions.
  • Targets: −30 °C to +70 °C, at least 3 kN compressive load, under $60 CAD per unit, at least 5 years of service life.
  • I produced the SolidWorks drawings: full assembly, mat, insulator panel, and reflector.
CourseMEC325, Fall 2025
TeamTeam 0305 (6)
ToolsSolidWorks, human-factors analysis
StatusComplete

Rubber-band powered acrylic car

A wind-up car cut from a single 8 × 10 in acrylic sheet. It has to knock over Jenga blocks along a course and stop inside a parking zone, using only a wooden dowel, five rubber bands, a paperclip, and a handful of screws.

  • Larger 2 in rear drive wheels (with cut rubber band tires for grip) and 1.5 in front wheels, on a press-fit dowel axle.
  • Two 10 in arms swing out after the car leaves the garage. A string limiter holds them at a height that skims the tops of the blocks to save energy.
  • Drive band anchored at the front and friction-wound onto the rear axle, so the tension is controlled and releases cleanly.
  • I produced the SolidWorks drawings and the exploded assembly view.
CourseMEC322, Winter 2026
Team5 students
ToolsSolidWorks, laser cutter
StatusBuilt and tested

MATLAB dynamics simulations

Two computer labs from MEC311 (Dynamics) where I derived the equations by hand and then implemented them in MATLAB.

Lab 1: end-effector motion planning
Compared a quadratic and a quartic speed profile between two points. The quartic profile finishes in about 1.6 s versus 2.8 s, but peaks near 58 cm/s² of acceleration versus about 35 cm/s², and the reaction force on a 0.3 kg end effector follows the same trend.
Lab 2: slider-crank engine
Free-body and kinetic diagrams for the connecting rod and piston, acceleration derivations for the crank at 1000 RPM (crank 0.06 m, rod 0.16 m, rod mass 3 kg, piston mass 2.5 kg), and plots of the pin reaction forces at B and D from 0° to 180°.
CourseMEC311
ToolsMATLAB
TopicsKinematics, kinetics
StatusComplete
